2. Data Categories, Sources and Data-Protection Roles

Depending on the contact or engagement, I process in particular:

  • identity and contact data such as your name, email address and postal address;
  • professional and organisational information such as your employer, position, function, team membership and professional context;
  • contract, billing, payment and tax data;
  • scheduling, communication and participation data;
  • content communicated or developed in coaching, interviews, team formats, workshops or offsites, including my session and working notes, agreed actions and approved outputs;
  • audio and transcript data only where recording or automated transcription has first been authorised;
  • technical and usage data generated when the website or communication, documentation or transcription services are used.

I receive data mainly from the individual concerned. In corporate engagements, names, business contact details, roles, team membership, scheduling information and information about the organisational context may also come from the commissioning organisation, a sponsor, an employer or another project participant. For participant and contact information supplied by a commissioning organisation, the information duties under Article 14 GDPR apply, generally within one month and earlier at the first communication or disclosure. A conversation may also contain incidental information about colleagues or other people. I limit such information to what is needed and avoid names and identifying details where unnecessary. I assess and document individually whether an Article 14 exception applies to a particular third party; coaching confidentiality alone is not a blanket exemption. Where no exception applies, I fulfil the applicable information duties. The commissioning organisation remains responsible for the lawfulness and transparency of its own disclosure.

As a freelance executive coach, I determine the content of individual coaching together with the coachee, independently of the sponsor. I am the controller for this confidential coaching. In corporate engagements, the commissioning organisation and I are generally separate controllers for our respective processing. Roles in a specific project follow the actual allocation of decisions and responsibilities, not a classification in a procurement system. Where I process data solely on the client’s documented instructions in a specific project, an agreement under Article 28 GDPR will be entered into before processing begins. Joint controllership will be regulated separately where required.

3. Purposes and Legal Bases

I process personal data for the following purposes and on the following legal bases:

  • enquiries, steps before entering into a contract, contract formation and performance of a contract with the person concerned: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR;
  • organisation and professional delivery of corporate engagements for coachees and participants who are not themselves contractual parties, project communication, confidential documentation and continuity of the services: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR;
  • creation, organisation and revision of ordinary prepared working notes and preparation and follow-up, excluding the consent-based processing described below: Article 6(1)(b) or (f) GDPR;
  • voluntary audio capture, automated transcription, AI-assisted conversation notes in Granola and optional retention of raw transcripts for the agreed coaching purpose: Article 6(1)(a) GDPR;
  • compliance with statutory retention, tax and documentation duties: Article 6(1)(c) GDPR;
  • establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.

My legitimate interests are reliable and consistently high-quality service delivery, secure project organisation and IT use, confidential documentation, and the establishment and defence of legal claims. In balancing interests, I take particular account of the individual’s confidentiality expectations, the sensitivity of the format, data minimisation, limited retention periods and human oversight. Special categories of personal data are additionally subject to section 11.

12. Retention and Deletion

Retention depends on a defined purpose and continuing necessity, not on using the longest conceivable period. There is no general statutory retention period for raw executive-coaching transcripts. The following rules apply to the copies under my control:

Lawful engagement-specific agreements may provide for shorter retention or additional deletion obligations, which I follow subject to applicable legal duties. Any longer retention requires a separately lawful, necessary purpose and transparent information; a contract alone does not justify it. Return or deletion arrangements do not authorise disclosure of confidential individual coaching records to a sponsor.

  • Audio: Granola states that temporary audio is deleted after transcription; it does not retain an audio archive.
  • Granola copies: Raw transcripts are deleted from Granola once the local export has been checked and they are no longer needed there for preparing the agreed notes. Granola notes and temporary AI drafts are deleted when they are no longer needed for preparation or follow-up. Granola is not used as a permanent archive; provider copies still needed during coaching are deleted no later than the end of the active relationship, subject only to the narrow exceptions below.
  • Local raw transcripts: With informed consent covering local retention, I may keep necessary text transcripts during the active coaching relationship to revisit relevant wording, context and agreed topics across sessions. This is not automatic retention of every complete transcript for the whole relationship. I review necessity when preparing later sessions and at least every three months; if final notes suffice, the raw transcript or unnecessary parts are deleted earlier. Remaining raw transcripts are deleted without undue delay when the active coaching relationship ends. A merely possible future return is not a reason to retain them.

An active coaching relationship ends when it is terminated or completed, or when further sessions or follow-up are no longer concretely planned or needed. At the three-month review, a pause is not treated as an indefinite extension: retention continues only if there is a specific agreed continuation and a documented need for the retained information.

  • Prepared notes and shared Google documents: These are kept during the engagement as needed for the agreed joint work. Afterwards, only necessary documentation is retained for a specific continuing purpose, including proportionate evidence of service delivery or defence of claims. The ordinary outer limit is 31 December of the third calendar year following the end of the engagement; unnecessary content is deleted earlier and sharing ends when no longer needed. This is my chosen retention limit with a necessity review, not a statutory duty to preserve intimate coaching notes. Raw transcripts are subject to the shorter rule above.
  • Enquiries that do not result in a contract are generally deleted no later than twelve months after correspondence ends.
  • Business and commercial correspondence is retained for six years where legally required, accounting records and invoices for eight years, and books, financial statements and comparable tax records for ten years. These periods apply only to the records covered by the relevant legal duty, not to coaching conversations or transcripts.
  • Specific legal exception: Only information demonstrably necessary for a concrete legal dispute, specifically anticipated claim or applicable legal duty may be retained beyond the ordinary rule, on a separately valid legal basis. Sensitive data additionally requires an Article 9 exception, such as Article 9(2)(f) where applicable. Retention is limited to relevant extracts where sufficient, restricted to that purpose and reviewed until the reason ends; it does not justify keeping every full transcript.

Withdrawal of consent ends the processing that depends on it. The affected raw transcripts and consent-only copies are deleted without undue delay unless a separately applicable legal obligation or concrete claims-related basis requires limited retention. I do not automatically switch to legitimate interests to retain previously consent-based raw transcripts. Withdrawal does not affect earlier lawful processing. Provider-side final erasure follows the applicable contractual deletion and backup cycles; deletion from an account and final erasure from all provider backups are not necessarily simultaneous. Google's DPA provides for deletion within 180 days after customer data becomes non-recoverable in the service. At the end of the contract with Google, an additional recovery period of up to 30 days may precede that period, subject to legal storage duties and the DPA’s exception for data also covered by another continuing agreement.

The rules apply to active local files and provider copies under my control. Any residual backup copies remain protected until their ordinary overwrite cycle and are not reused for coaching; deletion instructions also apply if a backup is restored. Copies independently made by a recipient are outside my control, and that recipient is responsible for their own subsequent use.

14. Your Data Protection Rights

Subject to the statutory conditions, you have the right of access under Article 15 GDPR, rectification under Article 16 GDPR, erasure under Article 17 GDPR, restriction of processing under Article 18 GDPR and data portability under Article 20 GDPR.

Right to object: Where processing is based on Article 6(1)(f) GDPR, you may object under Article 21 GDPR on grounds relating to your particular situation. I will then no longer process the relevant data unless I demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or the processing is required for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.

You may withdraw consent at any time with future effect. Requests are generally answered within one month. Where a request is particularly complex or numerous requests are received, this period may be extended by up to two further months where the statutory conditions are met.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data-protection supervisory authority. The supervisory authority generally responsible for supervising my processing is the Bavarian Data Protection Authority for the Private Sector (BayLDA), Promenade 18, 91522 Ansbach, Germany. You may also contact another competent supervisory authority.

15. Data Security

I use technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, including access restrictions, secured accounts, encrypted transmission channels and data minimisation.

Local transcripts are kept on my computer, separately from the prepared notes shared through Google Docs. Access to raw transcripts is limited to the confidential coaching purpose. I protect local files and backups with encryption, secure provider accounts with multi-factor authentication, and restrict and review access permissions for shared documents. These controls cover storage, transmission and recovery from backups.

Cloud processing is also protected by the providers’ contractual and technical safeguards. Despite appropriate measures, absolute protection cannot be guaranteed for electronic transmission or storage.
